# Editing Resources #07 — Sound Design & Audio Mixing

> Layer effects, voice, game audio and intentional silence without burying the information.

**Scope:** 36 practical techniques and editorial concepts, grouped into 3 sections. Some are established film/postproduction terms; others are explicitly descriptive workflows rather than formal industry jargon.

**Application:** Roblox / Anime Breaker Shorts, faceless gaming guides, tutorials and challenge videos.

## Essential sound effects

| Technique / method | Short explanation | Roblox or faceless-video use |
|---|---|---|
| **Whoosh** | Short air movement accent for a directional camera or text move | Place under a fast pan, not every cut |
| **Impact hit** | Brief percussive sound that emphasizes arrival or contact | Land on boss appearance or reward reveal |
| **Sub hit** | Deep low-frequency thump perceived more than heard on small speakers | Layer quietly beneath a boss-impact moment |
| **Riser** | Sound increasing in intensity toward a reveal | Build tension before the boss appears |
| **Downlifter** | Falling pitch/texture that signals a release | Use after a reward or montage peak |
| **Stinger** | Very short musical/sound punctuation | Emphasize a surprising discovery |
| **Tick** | Crisp micro-accent for UI and counters | Each important stat change can get one tick |
| **Click** | Tight mechanical tap | Highlight a code being copied |
| **Pop** | Round transient suited to playful captions | Reinforce one important word |
| **Reverse swell** | Sound reversed into a cut | Lead into a sudden boss reveal |
| **Swipe** | Soft textured sweep | Transition between map landmarks |
| **Glitch burst** | Short digital interference accent | Signal incorrect code or corrupted UI |

## Layering and mixing

| Technique / method | Short explanation | Roblox or faceless-video use |
|---|---|---|
| **Dialogue-first mix** | Set voiceover as the reference level before adding other audio | Guide information remains understandable |
| **Gain staging** | Keep individual clip levels below overload before final output | Avoid peaking when many effects hit together |
| **Peak control** | Reduce excessive transient spikes to prevent distortion | Lower layered impacts before exporting |
| **Audio ducking** | Lower music/game sound while narration speaks | Keep directions clear in a quick guide |
| **Crossfade** | Overlap fades to join two sound clips smoothly | Blend adjacent ambience regions |
| **Hard audio cut** | Deliberately stop a sound on a picture cut | Add surprise when switching to a new world |
| **Equalization** | Adjust frequency balance to reduce muddiness or harshness | Clean phone-recorded voice |
| **High-pass filter** | Reduce very low rumble | Tidy noisy room audio |
| **Noise reduction** | Suppress steady hiss carefully | Improve a phone-recorded guide |
| **Compression** | Reduce level differences across a signal | Keep quiet and loud voice phrases closer |
| **Limiter** | Catch peaks at the end of a mix | Help avoid clipping in export |
| **Stereo placement** | Spread noncritical effects left/right subtly | Keep voice centered and clear |

## Story and retention audio

| Technique / method | Short explanation | Roblox or faceless-video use |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-lap** | Start a sound for the coming shot before the image changes | Boss growl before revealing the boss |
| **J-cut** | Audio of the next shot begins early | Hear the new area while still seeing the route |
| **L-cut** | Continue audio of the old shot after the image changes | Keep narration flowing over a new camera view |
| **Room tone** | Quiet consistent ambient bed | Avoid a dead-silent gap after a cut |
| **Silence drop** | Briefly remove audio to heighten contrast | One beat before the boss hits |
| **Sound bridge** | One continuous sound joins visually distinct scenes | Music carries across different worlds |
| **Rhythmic footstep** | Match step sounds to visible walking | Sell movement through a location guide |
| **Layered impact** | Combine attack transient, sub and tail in balance | Make a finishing blow feel weighty |
| **Pitch sweep** | Gradual note or noise pitch change | Signal upgrade progression |
| **Reverb tail** | Short decay suggesting space | Give a portal or hall a sense of scale |
| **Dry/wet contrast** | Switch between unprocessed and reverberant audio | Emphasize entering a tunnel |
| **Loopable ambience** | Ambient sound whose beginning/end connect cleanly | Build continuous gameplay atmosphere |

## Ready-to-use example

Boss guide: dry voice hook -> quiet game ambience -> subtle whoosh while traveling -> small riser before reveal -> single impact hit -> voice-only health/drop explanation.

## Avoid these mistakes

- Adding every sound effect at full volume
- Letting music compete with important numbers
- Confusing loudness with excitement
- Using copyrighted tracks without permission

## Final review checklist

- [ ] Can I understand every word on a phone speaker?
- [ ] Does each big sound match a visible event?
- [ ] Are transitions smooth unless an abrupt cut is intentional?
- [ ] Does any peak distort?

## Working notes

- In CapCut, exact feature names and availability can vary by platform and app version.
- A digital crop, blur or keyframe animation may simulate a movement but cannot reconstruct missing 3D camera perspective.
- For stats, codes and release-specific claims, verify the footage/game before publishing.
- Use original, licensed or otherwise permitted audio and visual assets.
