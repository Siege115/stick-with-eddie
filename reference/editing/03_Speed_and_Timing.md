# Editing Resources: Speed & Timing

**234 named techniques and concepts across 12 sections**

For faceless edits, Roblox gameplay, YouTube Shorts, TikTok, and general cinematic editing. This reference is intentionally about **time, rhythm, and pacing** - not camera angles, backgrounds, or camera movements.

> Some names are established filmmaking terms; others are descriptive creator/editing shorthand. Closely related terms are included intentionally because editors and tutorials use different wording.

## Quick-start: five techniques in the video

1. **Slow motion:** slow an attack wind-up to focus attention.
2. **Speed ramp:** accelerate movement, then slow near a key event.
3. **Freeze frame:** hold a specific action pose or readable stat.
4. **Jump cut:** skip travel or repetitive actions instantly.
5. **Impact cut:** switch visuals or accent a frame on the hit.

## CapCut recipes (iPhone)

- **Slow motion:** select clip -> Speed -> Normal -> try 0.5x; check whether the original footage remains smooth.
- **Speed ramp:** select clip -> Speed -> Curve -> choose a preset or Custom; keep the fastest section on travel and slow down near the payoff.
- **Freeze frame:** select clip -> Freeze (if available in your version); extend the still, add text or an arrow, then resume footage.
- **Jump cut:** Split before and after boring movement, delete the middle, close the gap.
- **Impact cut:** split at the exact strike/contact frame; cut to a closer or result shot and optionally add a 1-3-frame flash plus a short impact sound.

**Frame-rate reality check:** a 30-fps source slowed to 0.25x may stutter without interpolation. Interpolated frames can introduce artifacts around Roblox swords, fast particles, and UI.

## 01. Playback speed and duration

Basic retiming controls: how long a moment lasts onscreen.

- **Normal speed** - Original real-time playback, usually 1x.
- **Fast motion** - Increase playback speed to compress routine actions.
- **Slow motion** - Reduce playback speed to emphasize motion or reaction.
- **Half speed** - 0.5x playback; needs sufficient source frames to remain smooth.
- **Quarter speed** - 0.25x playback; smoothness depends heavily on footage frame rate.
- **Double speed** - 2x playback; useful for travel or repetitive gameplay.
- **Triple speed** - 3x playback; compresses long low-value sequences.
- **High-speed playback** - 4x or faster to skip over low-information gameplay.
- **Variable speed** - Different playback rates within the same clip.
- **Constant-speed retime** - One uniform speed for an entire selected section.
- **Duration trim** - Shorten a clip without changing the playback rate.
- **Time compression** - Fit more real action into less screen time.
- **Time expansion** - Make a brief source moment occupy longer screen time.
- **Real-time hold** - Stay on a moment at normal pace for clarity.
- **Temporal emphasis** - Spend extra time on the most important action.
- **Temporal contrast** - Put fast and slow sections side by side for impact.
- **Duration match** - Set a clip to a target length, retiming if required.
- **Timing offset** - Shift a shot or event earlier or later in the timeline.

## 02. Speed ramps and curve shapes

Speed changes within a shot, including common and descriptive curve types.

- **Speed ramp** - Smoothly accelerate or decelerate playback within a clip.
- **Ramp in** - Begin at one rate and progressively speed up.
- **Ramp out** - Gradually slow down toward a key moment or ending.
- **Slow-to-fast ramp** - Start slow, then accelerate into movement.
- **Fast-to-slow ramp** - Start fast, then decelerate before a reveal.
- **Slow-fast-slow ramp** - Highlight takeoff, rush through movement, then land slowly.
- **Fast-slow-fast ramp** - Briefly emphasize a central action, then regain pace.
- **Speed burst** - Short high-speed section surrounded by normal playback.
- **Speed dip** - Brief slowdown around a hit, reveal, or decision.
- **Speed pulse** - Repeating bursts and slowdowns to create a rhythmic feel.
- **Ease-in speed change** - Start accelerating gently instead of abruptly.
- **Ease-out speed change** - Arrive at the target speed gently.
- **Linear speed ramp** - Playback speed changes at a steady rate.
- **S-curve ramp** - Ease into and out of the speed transition.
- **Bezier speed curve** - Fine-tune ramp rate using custom curve handles.
- **Step speed change** - Switch immediately from one rate to another.
- **Plateau ramp** - Accelerate, hold a fast rate, then decelerate.
- **Asymmetric ramp** - Accelerate faster than you decelerate, or vice versa.
- **Acceleration hold** - Keep increasing speed through the action.
- **Deceleration hold** - Ease down gradually toward a critical frame.
- **Reverse speed ramp** - Combine speed variation with reversed playback.
- **Ramp-to-freeze** - Slow rapidly into a deliberate freeze frame.
- **Ramp-from-freeze** - Accelerate out of a still hold.
- **Beat-driven ramp** - Place speed inflection points on music or impact beats.

## 03. Slow-motion methods

Capture and interpolation concepts that determine slow-motion quality.

- **Overcranked footage** - Capture at high fps, then play back at a lower timeline fps.
- **Native slow motion** - Smooth slowdown using frames genuinely recorded by the camera/game capture.
- **Conformed frame rate** - Interpret high-fps footage at a lower playback rate for slow motion.
- **50 percent conform** - Example: 60 fps footage on a 30 fps timeline yields half-speed playback.
- **Frame duplication** - Reuse existing frames to extend time; can look choppy.
- **Frame blending** - Mix adjacent frames to soften retimed movement; may ghost.
- **Optical flow** - Estimate motion between frames to create intermediate frames.
- **AI frame interpolation** - Generate missing frames using a learned motion model.
- **Motion-compensated interpolation** - Synthesize in-betweens using estimated object motion.
- **Ghosting artifact** - Doubled edges caused by blending or poor interpolation.
- **Warping artifact** - Bent or melting shapes caused by failed motion estimates.
- **Judder** - Uneven or stuttering motion during frame-rate conversion.
- **Frame sampling** - Pick source frames at intervals when retiming.
- **Shutter blur continuity** - Keep motion blur visually consistent through speed changes.
- **Slow-motion reaction** - Extend the character's response after an important event.
- **Slow-motion anticipation** - Extend the wind-up before an attack or reveal.
- **Slow-motion payoff** - Extend the moment a reward, hit, or surprise becomes visible.
- **Micro slow-mo** - Very short slowdown, often only a few frames.

## 04. Freeze frames and holds

Ways to stop, suspend, or emphasize a specific frame.

- **Freeze frame** - Hold one chosen frame as a still image.
- **Hold frame** - Extend a selected frame for extra reading time.
- **Frame hold** - The same concept as hold frame in many editors.
- **Stop-frame effect** - Suddenly stop moving footage for emphasis.
- **Freeze-frame intro** - Introduce a subject while the image is paused.
- **Freeze-frame outro** - End on a still for a punchline or conclusion.
- **Freeze with label** - Pause while a name, stat, or arrow appears.
- **Freeze with zoom** - Move or crop a still while source action stays paused.
- **Freeze and outline** - Add an outline around the subject in a paused frame.
- **Freeze with cutout** - Separate a frozen subject from the background.
- **Freeze with background blur** - Keep the subject readable by softening surroundings.
- **Freeze-frame flash** - Briefly insert a held still as an accent.
- **Stutter freeze** - Alternate short holds with small motion advances.
- **Hit-stop** - Pause action for a few frames at an impact, common in games/anime.
- **Impact hold** - Extend the impact pose after contact.
- **Pause-resume** - Halt action, then return to running footage.
- **Time-stop illusion** - Freeze most of a scene while selected elements still animate.

## 05. Reverses, loops and time tricks

Temporal effects that alter event order or repeat actions.

- **Reverse playback** - Run the source clip backward.
- **Reverse-speed ramp** - Retimed reverse sequence, often used for rewind moments.
- **Rewind effect** - Repeated backward motion with visual or audio cues.
- **Reverse-to-forward** - Show a moment backward, then replay it normally.
- **Forward-reverse loop** - Play an action one way, then in reverse.
- **Boomerang loop** - Forward and backward playback for a seamless bounce.
- **Ping-pong loop** - Repeat forward/back cycles at clip boundaries.
- **Action replay** - Repeat an important moment for emphasis.
- **Instant replay** - Cut back and show a just-seen action again.
- **Slow-motion replay** - Revisit a moment in slow motion for clarity.
- **Multi-angle replay** - Re-show one event from different recorded views.
- **Frame echo** - Trail repeated earlier frames behind the subject.
- **Stroboscopic echo** - Show motion as spaced repeated positions.
- **Time echo** - Overlay frames from neighboring moments.
- **Time remap** - Use a controllable mapping between source time and timeline time.
- **Temporal displacement** - Offset different visual layers to different moments.
- **Time-loop reveal** - Repeat a short event before a payoff or twist.
- **Time-lapse** - Capture or sample frames over time to speed long change.
- **Hyperlapse** - Time-lapse with substantial moving-camera position change.

## 06. Cuts and temporal continuity

How edit points control perceived speed and seamlessness.

- **Straight cut** - Switch immediately from one shot to the next.
- **Hard cut** - An abrupt cut without a transition effect.
- **Jump cut** - Skip forward within similar framing to compress time.
- **Smash cut** - Sudden cut to a sharply contrasting image or sound.
- **Match-on-action cut** - Cut between views during the same ongoing movement.
- **Cut on movement** - Hide a cut during significant on-screen motion.
- **Cut on impact** - Switch shots exactly as contact is made.
- **Cut on the beat** - Put the cut at an audible musical pulse.
- **Cut before the beat** - Lead the beat slightly to create anticipation.
- **Cut after the beat** - Let the musical hit land before switching image.
- **Cut on reaction** - Change shots when a character responds.
- **Cut to the result** - Skip the middle action and show its outcome.
- **Elliptical cut** - Deliberately omit part of elapsed time.
- **Continuity cut** - Preserve natural time, space, and direction between shots.
- **Discontinuity cut** - Deliberately break continuity for surprise or energy.
- **Overlap edit** - Repeat a small bit of action across the edit for emphasis.
- **Action overlap** - Show a moment of movement twice across adjacent shots.
- **Temporal match cut** - Connect actions at similar phases of their movement.
- **Cutaway** - Momentarily show a related detail, then return to the main shot.
- **Insert shot** - Brief close view of a crucial object, UI, or action.
- **Reaction insert** - Very short shot showing emotional response to an event.
- **Flash cut** - Insert a momentary single- or few-frame image.

## 07. Rhythm and music synchronization

Methods for organizing edits around audio events.

- **Beat sync** - Align important visual events with music pulses.
- **Downbeat cut** - Cut on a strong beat at the beginning of a measure.
- **Upbeat cut** - Cut on a lighter offbeat to keep a sequence lively.
- **Offbeat cut** - Place a cut between main beats for syncopation.
- **Backbeat sync** - Accentuate common snare beats in a groove.
- **Half-time editing** - Cut at larger beat intervals for a spacious feel.
- **Double-time editing** - Cut twice as often as the main beat suggests.
- **Quarter-note pacing** - One visual change per beat in common meter.
- **Eighth-note pacing** - Two changes per beat for rapid energy.
- **Sixteenth-note burst** - A very fast flurry of cuts on subdivisions.
- **Syncopated editing** - Alternate expected and unexpected edit placements.
- **Onset matching** - Align a cut to a sound's initial attack.
- **Transient sync** - Land a visual change on a sharp audio transient.
- **Phrase-based cutting** - Change scenes at the end/start of musical phrases.
- **Bar-based montage** - Organize shot patterns into complete measures.
- **Beat-drop reveal** - Show the key image at a music drop.
- **Riser-to-drop timing** - Build anticipation during a rise and release at the drop.
- **Musical stinger** - Use a short accent to punctuate a cut or reveal.
- **Rhythmic repetition** - Repeat cut intervals to establish a pattern.
- **Rhythmic break** - Interrupt that pattern to highlight an exception.
- **Audio-led cut** - Let sound dictate the exact edit point.
- **Visual-led sync** - Choose a visual moment first and fit sound to it.

## 08. Transition duration and reveal timing

The timing of transitions matters as much as their appearance.

- **Whip-pan cut timing** - Place a cut during peak rapid pan blur.
- **Zoom transition timing** - Match cut point to maximum zoom energy.
- **Motion-match transition** - Join shots with compatible motion direction and speed.
- **Match-frame transition** - Find visually similar frames on either side of a cut.
- **Speed-ramp transition** - Hide a cut in a fast retimed movement.
- **Flash transition** - Use a very brief bright frame to bridge two shots.
- **Black-frame transition** - Insert a brief dark interval as rhythmic punctuation.
- **White-frame transition** - Insert bright frames as a stylized accent.
- **Blink transition** - Make a brief dark-out look like an eyelid closing.
- **Whip blur bridge** - Use quick blur over the edit to mask scene change.
- **Quick dissolve** - Very short overlap to soften an otherwise hard cut.
- **Long dissolve** - Extended overlap that conveys passage or reflection.
- **L-cut** - Let outgoing audio continue under the next picture.
- **J-cut** - Bring incoming audio in before the next picture appears.
- **Pre-lap sound** - Hear an upcoming scene before seeing it.
- **Post-lap sound** - Let a previous scene's sound continue into the next.
- **Transition tail** - Allow a visual effect to settle after the main cut.

## 09. Action, impact and game timing

Timing tricks suited to gameplay, action clips, and short guides.

- **Anticipation hold** - Pause just before an attack to build expectation.
- **Wind-up compression** - Shorten an attack's repetitive preparation.
- **Hit-stop** - Stop motion briefly when the attack connects.
- **Impact flash** - Place a 1-3 frame light accent on contact.
- **Impact shake timing** - Trigger camera shake precisely at the hit.
- **Impact zoom timing** - Reach the punch-in's largest size at contact.
- **Follow-through hold** - Keep the viewer on the aftermath before cutting.
- **Micro-replay** - Re-show a critical hit for a split second.
- **Delayed reveal** - Hold back the result for a beat to create suspense.
- **Instant reveal** - Show the most interesting result immediately.
- **Pre-hit silence** - Drop sound just before impact to create contrast.
- **Post-hit silence** - Momentarily remove audio after a major event.
- **One-frame accent** - Use a single visual frame as a jolt, if encoding allows.
- **Three-frame accent** - Brief enough to feel punchy but more visible than one frame.
- **Frame-step anticipation** - Advance in tiny increments before the payoff.
- **Attack-to-reward cut** - Skip repetitive combat and jump to the drop/reward.
- **Enemy spawn cut** - Cut directly from locating the spawn to its appearance.
- **Boss reveal timing** - Reveal the enemy just after a question or direction cue.
- **UI stat hold** - Keep a damage or health number readable for the viewer.
- **Gameplay dead-air trim** - Remove waiting, walking, loading, and menus with no new info.

## 10. Narrative pace and short-form retention

Editorial pacing concepts, not literal speed effects.

- **Cold open** - Begin with the strongest action before exposition.
- **Hook timing** - Show a clear promise or curiosity trigger right away.
- **Open loop** - Establish a question that the rest of the clip answers.
- **Payoff timing** - Deliver the promised result before attention fades.
- **Rapid setup** - Compress setup into the minimum useful context.
- **Breathing room** - Brief calm moment to let important information register.
- **Information density** - Adjust how many new facts appear per second.
- **Visual reset** - Change composition or scene after an information-heavy section.
- **Pattern interrupt** - Break established rhythm to restore attention.
- **Escalating pace** - Increase shot frequency or action intensity over time.
- **De-escalating pace** - Slow editing after the main action to aid comprehension.
- **Pacing contrast** - Alternate quiet explanation and fast demonstration.
- **Accelerating montage** - Make successive cuts shorter to build urgency.
- **Decelerating montage** - Lengthen successive clips to convey resolution.
- **Time budget** - Allocate seconds intentionally among hook, setup, proof, payoff.
- **Retention edit** - Remove stretches that do not add clarity or momentum.
- **Reveal-first structure** - Start with the outcome, then explain the steps.
- **Cause-effect order** - Show the action and its consequence in readable order.
- **Micro-cliffhanger** - Briefly withhold an answer at a scene boundary.
- **Narration-led pacing** - Set edit points so the footage illustrates the voice.
- **Caption dwell time** - Hold on-screen text long enough to read comfortably.

## 11. Sound and visual timing

Syncing effects, voice, captions, and music to picture.

- **Whoosh synchronization** - Peak a swoosh near the fastest visible movement.
- **Impact synchronization** - Align the sound's attack with the hit frame.
- **Sound bridge** - Use continuous audio to join shots smoothly.
- **Audio ducking** - Lower music around important speech or impact sounds.
- **Sidechain ducking** - Automatically reduce one audio source when another becomes loud.
- **Music cut-out** - Silence music briefly for a joke, hit, or reveal.
- **Riser onset** - Start a rising cue early enough to build toward the reveal.
- **Bass drop accent** - Place a low-frequency hit under a major visual change.
- **Audio pre-roll** - Begin the sound slightly before the visible event.
- **Audio post-roll** - Allow a sound's decay to continue after the image changes.
- **SFX tail preservation** - Avoid chopping off an impact's natural decay.
- **Silence beat** - Briefly remove most sound to reset viewer expectation.
- **Voice emphasis cut** - Align a new shot with the spoken keyword.
- **Caption-pop sync** - Animate key words as they are said.
- **Word-by-word captions** - Reveal subtitles in step with spoken words.
- **Syllable sync** - Time tiny visual accents to a syllable for a punchline.
- **Lip-sync timing** - Match mouth shapes to speech when a visible character talks.
- **Waveform editing** - Use peaks in an audio waveform to place exact cut points.

## 12. Workflow and technical timing

Production terms that help produce clean, predictable retiming.

- **Timeline frame rate** - Frames per second used for the finished edit.
- **Source frame rate** - Frames per second actually recorded in the source clip.
- **Frame-accurate cut** - Place a cut at an exact frame boundary.
- **Timeline markers** - Save beat, voice, and effect locations before editing.
- **Beat markers** - Mark recurring pulse locations to align cuts.
- **Keyframe** - A point storing an animated property at a specific time.
- **Keyframe spacing** - Distance between animation points; changes motion timing.
- **Keyframe easing** - Control how motion speeds up or slows between keyframes.
- **Retime keyframe** - Define a source-to-timeline speed or timing change.
- **Nested retime** - Apply timing to a compound clip or pre-composed sequence.
- **Slip edit** - Change which source frames appear without moving the clip's timeline slot.
- **Slide edit** - Move a clip along the timeline while compensating adjacent edits.
- **Ripple trim** - Change one clip length and shift later clips accordingly.
- **Roll edit** - Move an edit boundary while preserving the two-clip total duration.
- **Timecode** - Hour-minute-second-frame address for precise editing.
- **Frame stepping** - Move one frame at a time to find the contact point.
- **Proxy workflow** - Use lower-resolution media for smoother editing previews.
- **Export frame-rate check** - Confirm final fps, duration, and sync after rendering.

## Anime Breaker editing templates

**Secret boss reveal (15 seconds):** 0:00-0:02 show the boss (hook); 0:02-0:05 location landmark; 0:05-0:09 jump-cut through travel; 0:09-0:11 slow/freeze near the correct turn; 0:11-0:15 reveal and hold on boss name or reward.

**High-damage moment (10 seconds):** 0:00-0:02 show the target; 0:02-0:04 slow the attack wind-up; 0:04-0:05 speed up movement into the strike; 0:05 hit-stop for 2-4 frames and sync an impact; 0:05-0:08 hold the damage number; 0:08-0:10 replay the hit or show the final result.

**Codes guide (12 seconds):** 0:00-0:01 urgency hook; 0:01-0:03 skip menu travel; 0:03-0:10 show codes long enough to pause/read (avoid flashy transitions); 0:10-0:12 show where to redeem.

## Practical reminders

- **Clarity beats effects:** do not accelerate the only moment viewers need to understand.
- **Don't overuse freeze frames:** use them for emphasis, directions, readable labels, or humor.
- **Avoid false smoothness claims:** interpolation cannot reconstruct every motion correctly.
- **Check the final exported video:** 30 fps means one frame is 33.3 milliseconds; a one-frame flash may disappear on some playback/compression.
- **Use speed and timing with purpose:** every pause, burst, and cut should clarify or heighten the action.
