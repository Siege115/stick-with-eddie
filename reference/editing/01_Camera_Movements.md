# Editing Resources — Camera Movements

A searchable reference of **camera movements only**. Each entry gives the common name (or a clearly described variation) and a short definition. Some movements are physical camera moves; others use lens zooms or are simulated in editing. Related names can overlap in filmmaking.

## 1. Essential linear camera moves

1. **Dolly in / Push in** — Move the camera toward the subject.
2. **Dolly out / Pullback** — Move the camera away from the subject.
3. **Truck left** — Move the entire camera left without merely turning it.
4. **Truck right** — Move the entire camera right.
5. **Pedestal up** — Raise the camera vertically while keeping its angle.
6. **Pedestal down** — Lower the camera vertically while keeping its angle.
7. **Crane up** — Lift the camera on a crane or jib, often along a curved path.
8. **Crane down** — Lower the camera on a crane or jib.
9. **Boom up** — Raise a camera using a boom/jib mechanism (related to crane up).
10. **Boom down** — Lower a camera using a boom/jib mechanism.
11. **Slider left** — Short, smooth leftward lateral camera movement.
12. **Slider right** — Short, smooth rightward lateral camera movement.
13. **Slider push** — Short, smooth forward camera movement.
14. **Slider pull** — Short, smooth backward camera movement.
15. **Diagonal push in** — Move forward and sideways at the same time.
16. **Diagonal pullback** — Move backward and sideways at the same time.
17. **Diagonal rise** — Move sideways/forward while gaining height.
18. **Diagonal descent** — Move sideways/forward while losing height.
19. **Curved dolly** — Move the camera along a curved track.
20. **Lateral tracking move** — Travel sideways beside a subject.
21. **Forward tracking move** — Travel forward with a moving subject.
22. **Backward tracking move** — Travel backward in front of a moving subject.
23. **Vertical tracking move** — Track a moving subject upward or downward.
24. **Pass-by move** — Move the camera past a stationary subject.
25. **Overtake move** — Accelerate past a subject traveling in the same direction.

## 2. Camera rotations and aiming moves

26. **Pan left** — Rotate the camera horizontally to the left from its position.
27. **Pan right** — Rotate the camera horizontally to the right.
28. **Tilt up** — Rotate the camera vertically upward.
29. **Tilt down** — Rotate the camera vertically downward.
30. **Roll clockwise** — Rotate the camera around its viewing axis clockwise.
31. **Roll counterclockwise** — Rotate around the viewing axis counterclockwise.
32. **Whip pan left** — Very fast pan to the left, often with motion blur.
33. **Whip pan right** — Very fast pan to the right.
34. **Whip tilt up** — Fast upward tilt.
35. **Whip tilt down** — Fast downward tilt.
36. **Snap pan** — Abrupt, precise re-aiming to another subject.
37. **Snap tilt** — Abrupt tilt to a new framing.
38. **Slow reveal pan** — Gradual horizontal turn that exposes new information.
39. **Slow reveal tilt** — Gradual vertical turn that exposes new information.
40. **180-degree pan** — Turn the camera through half a circle.
41. **360-degree pan** — Turn the camera through a full circle from one position.
42. **Continuous panoramic pan** — Keep rotating horizontally across a broad view.
43. **Pan-and-tilt** — Turn horizontally and vertically at the same time.
44. **Pan-and-roll** — Pan while rotating the camera's horizon.
45. **Tilt-and-roll** — Tilt while rotating the camera's horizon.
46. **Dutch roll** — Rotate the horizon into or out of a tilted angle.
47. **Pendulum tilt** — Rock the camera up and down rhythmically.
48. **Scanning pan** — Sweep across different points of interest in sequence.
49. **Reframing pan** — Small horizontal adjustment to keep a subject framed.
50. **Reframing tilt** — Small vertical adjustment to keep a subject framed.

## 3. Lens zooms and combined camera/zoom moves

51. **Optical zoom in** — Increase focal length so the subject fills more of the frame.
52. **Optical zoom out** — Decrease focal length so the frame widens.
53. **Slow creep zoom in** — Barely perceptible, gradual zoom toward a subject.
54. **Slow creep zoom out** — Barely perceptible, gradual zoom away.
55. **Crash zoom in** — Sudden, dramatic optical zoom inward.
56. **Crash zoom out** — Sudden, dramatic optical zoom outward.
57. **Snap zoom** — Very quick zoom to a preselected framing.
58. **Zoom pulse** — Zoom in and then out in one brief motion.
59. **Zoom oscillation** — Repeat small in/out zoom changes.
60. **Dolly zoom / Vertigo shot** — Dolly and zoom in opposite directions so the subject stays approximately the same size while background perspective changes.
61. **Dolly back + zoom in** — Classic dolly-zoom direction: retreat while increasing focal length.
62. **Dolly forward + zoom out** — Reverse dolly-zoom direction: approach while widening the lens.
63. **Push in + zoom in** — Physically move closer while also zooming tighter.
64. **Pullback + zoom out** — Physically move back while also widening the lens.
65. **Push in + zoom out** — Move closer while widening the lens (may be tuned into a dolly zoom).
66. **Pullback + zoom in** — Move back while zooming tighter (may be tuned into a dolly zoom).
67. **Zoom-and-pan** — Change focal length while panning.
68. **Zoom-and-tilt** — Change focal length while tilting.
69. **Zoom-and-roll** — Change focal length while rolling the camera.
70. **Ramped zoom** — Change zoom speed during the movement.

## 4. Subject-following and tracking patterns

71. **Following shot** — Camera moves behind a subject in the same direction.
72. **Leading shot** — Camera moves ahead of a subject while facing back at them.
73. **Side-follow shot** — Camera travels beside the subject.
74. **Parallel tracking shot** — Camera maintains a parallel path to the subject.
75. **Head-on tracking shot** — Camera retreats as the subject approaches.
76. **Rear tracking shot** — Camera advances behind a subject.
77. **Over-the-shoulder tracking** — Move while framing past the subject's shoulder.
78. **POV tracking** — Moving camera mimics what a character sees while traveling.
79. **Walk-and-talk tracking** — Move alongside or in front of walking speakers.
80. **Chase camera** — Camera pursues an accelerating or escaping subject.
81. **Vehicle-mounted tracking** — Camera moves with a vehicle or mounted platform.
82. **Car-to-car tracking** — Camera on one vehicle tracks another vehicle.
83. **Running follow** — Move after a running subject, often with pronounced kinetic motion.
84. **Low tracking shot** — Follow from close to the ground.
85. **High tracking shot** — Follow from above normal eye level.
86. **Close tracking shot** — Stay close to the moving subject.
87. **Wide tracking shot** — Maintain a more distant view of the subject and surroundings.
88. **Lead-then-pass tracking** — Start ahead of a subject and allow them to pass the camera.
89. **Follow-then-overtake tracking** — Begin behind a subject and travel past them.
90. **Crossing track** — Move across the subject's direction of travel.
91. **Intersecting track** — Camera and subject cross paths.
92. **Steadicam follow** — Stabilized body-mounted follow movement.
93. **Gimbal follow** — Motor-stabilized follow movement.
94. **Handheld follow** — Follow with natural handheld sway.
95. **Shoulder-mounted tracking** — Follow with a camera supported on the operator's shoulder.

## 5. Arcs, orbits, circles, and spirals

96. **Arc shot** — Move the camera along part of a circle around the subject.
97. **Quarter orbit (90°)** — Travel about a quarter-circle around the subject.
98. **Half orbit (180°)** — Travel about a semicircle around the subject.
99. **Full orbit (360°)** — Travel all the way around the subject.
100. **Clockwise orbit** — Circle the subject clockwise.
101. **Counterclockwise orbit** — Circle the subject counterclockwise.
102. **Tight orbit** — Circle close to the subject.
103. **Wide orbit** — Circle the subject from a greater radius.
104. **Orbit while pushing in** — Move around the subject while getting closer.
105. **Orbit while pulling out** — Move around the subject while getting farther away.
106. **Rising orbit** — Circle while gaining elevation.
107. **Descending orbit** — Circle while losing elevation.
108. **Spiral in** — Move inward along a spiral path.
109. **Spiral out** — Move outward along a spiral path.
110. **Helical rise** — Circle a subject while moving steadily upward.
111. **Helical descent** — Circle a subject while moving steadily downward.
112. **Corkscrew move** — Combine forward/backward translation with camera roll; sometimes also an orbit.
113. **Wraparound reveal** — Arc around a foreground object to uncover a subject behind it.
114. **Orbit-to-follow** — Transition from circling the subject into tracking behind them.
115. **Follow-to-orbit** — Transition from tracking a subject into circling them.

## 6. Crane, aerial, and overhead paths

116. **Jib rise** — Smooth lift using a jib arm.
117. **Jib drop** — Smooth lowering using a jib arm.
118. **Crane reveal** — Rise or move across an obstacle to reveal what is behind it.
119. **Crane over** — Carry the camera up and over a subject or obstacle.
120. **Crane swoop** — Curved crane movement that rises or falls while moving horizontally.
121. **Crane rise with tilt down** — Rise while aiming the lens progressively downward.
122. **Crane descent with tilt up** — Lower the camera while aiming upward.
123. **Drone ascent** — Fly straight up.
124. **Drone descent** — Fly straight down.
125. **Drone push-in** — Fly toward a subject.
126. **Drone pullback** — Fly away from a subject.
127. **Drone strafe left** — Fly sideways to the left while maintaining orientation.
128. **Drone strafe right** — Fly sideways to the right while maintaining orientation.
129. **Drone orbit** — Fly a circle around a subject.
130. **Drone rising orbit** — Circle while climbing.
131. **Drone descending orbit** — Circle while dropping altitude.
132. **Drone flyover** — Travel across and above a subject.
133. **Drone pass-through** — Fly through an opening or gap when safely achievable.
134. **Drone dive** — Descend rapidly toward a target or along a steep path.
135. **Drone pull-up** — Transition from a descending path into a climb.
136. **Drone chase** — Follow a moving subject from the air.
137. **Top-down travel** — Point straight down and translate over the ground.
138. **Bird's-eye drift** — Slow movement from a high, downward-looking camera position.
139. **Aerial reveal** — Rise or fly past an obstruction to expose a new scene.
140. **Aerial retreat-and-rise** — Fly backward and upward simultaneously.

## 7. Reveals, passages, and complex travel paths

141. **Push-through** — Move through foreground elements toward the background.
142. **Pull-through** — Back out through foreground elements.
143. **Doorway move-through** — Pass the camera through a doorway into another space.
144. **Window move-through** — Move through a window opening.
145. **Tunnel fly-through** — Travel continuously through a tunnel or narrow passage.
146. **Corner reveal** — Track around a corner to uncover the next space.
147. **Foreground parallax slide** — Slide sideways so near and far objects shift at different rates.
148. **Foreground parallax push** — Push forward so nearby objects move across the frame faster than the background.
149. **Obstruction reveal** — Move past a blocking object to uncover the subject.
150. **Pullback reveal** — Retreat until the wider setting or hidden context becomes visible.
151. **Tilt-up reveal** — Start low and tilt to expose a subject above.
152. **Tilt-down reveal** — Start high and tilt to expose a subject below.
153. **Rise-and-reveal** — Lift over a foreground obstacle.
154. **Drop-and-reveal** — Lower beneath an obstruction or into a new viewpoint.
155. **S-curve tracking path** — Travel along an S-shaped route.
156. **Zigzag tracking path** — Change lateral direction in successive segments.
157. **Serpentine tracking path** — Weave smoothly left and right while moving forward.
158. **Push-in then orbit** — Approach a subject and transition into an arc around them.
159. **Orbit then pullback** — Circle the subject and retreat into a wider view.
160. **Continuous one-take camera move** — Chain multiple paths together without a visible cut.

## 8. Expressive, stylized, and time-manipulated movements

161. **Handheld drift** — Loose, subtle position changes created by handheld operation.
162. **Handheld sway** — Gentle side-to-side rocking while maintaining the subject.
163. **Handheld shake** — Deliberately irregular camera motion.
164. **Micro-jitter** — Very small, rapid shaking.
165. **Floating camera** — Smooth, gently drifting movement with little visible vibration.
166. **Pendulum swing** — Swing the camera side to side along an arc.
167. **Snap push-in** — Very quick forward camera movement.
168. **Snap pullback** — Very quick retreat.
169. **Crash dolly in** — Aggressive, fast dolly movement toward a subject.
170. **Crash dolly out** — Aggressive, fast dolly movement away from a subject.
171. **Speed-ramped push-in** — Accelerate or decelerate a forward movement in playback.
172. **Speed-ramped pullback** — Accelerate or decelerate a backward movement in playback.
173. **Speed-ramped orbit** — Change an orbit's apparent speed in playback.
174. **Hyperlapse walk-through** — Travel a route while using interval capture/stabilization for accelerated movement.
175. **Hyperlapse orbit** — Create a time-compressed circular path around a subject.
176. **Time-lapse pan** — Pan slowly during interval capture; playback makes time and movement accelerate.
177. **Time-lapse tilt** — Tilt slowly during interval capture.
178. **Time-lapse slider move** — Short linear movement performed over a long capture period.
179. **Bullet-time orbit** — Apparent camera orbit around nearly frozen action, often made using multiple cameras or VFX.
180. **Repeatable motion-control move** — Robotic camera path recorded and repeated for multiple takes.

## 9. Game camera and virtual-production movements

181. **Freecam fly-through** — Move a virtual camera freely through a 3D scene.
182. **Freecam push-in** — Move a virtual camera straight toward a subject.
183. **Freecam pullback** — Move a virtual camera backward to widen context.
184. **Freecam strafe** — Slide a virtual camera left or right.
185. **Freecam ascend** — Raise a virtual camera without changing its aim.
186. **Freecam descend** — Lower a virtual camera.
187. **Spectator orbit** — Circle a player or object with a spectator camera.
188. **Third-person chase camera** — Follow behind and above a moving character.
189. **First-person head-bob movement** — Small walking/running oscillations on a first-person camera.
190. **Lock-on orbit** — Circle while automatically keeping a subject centered.
191. **Target-switch snap** — Rapidly redirect camera aim between targets.
192. **Camera-lag follow** — Let the camera trail or catch up after the subject moves.
193. **Spring-arm follow** — Follow with a virtual spring-like smoothing response.
194. **Spline-path fly-through** — Move a virtual camera along a predefined smooth curve.
195. **Rail-camera movement** — Constrain the virtual camera to a designed track.
196. **Scripted cinematic track** — Program the virtual camera to follow a timed route.
197. **Noclip pass-through** — Move a virtual camera through geometry without collisions.
198. **Terrain-following flyover** — Keep a virtual camera at roughly constant height above terrain.
199. **Isometric orbit** — Rotate an elevated, angled game camera around a scene.
200. **Shoulder-switch camera move** — Shift a third-person camera between left and right shoulder positions.

## 10. Camera movements recreated in editing (not physical camera travel)

201. **Digital push-in / Punch-in** — Keyframe crop/scale to move visually closer.
202. **Digital pullback** — Keyframe crop/scale to move visually farther away.
203. **Digital pan left** — Keyframe image position to shift framing left.
204. **Digital pan right** — Keyframe image position to shift framing right.
205. **Digital tilt up** — Keyframe position to reveal higher parts of a still or clip.
206. **Digital tilt down** — Keyframe position to reveal lower parts.
207. **Digital pedestal up** — Simulate a vertical camera rise with position and layers.
208. **Digital pedestal down** — Simulate a vertical camera drop.
209. **Digital roll clockwise** — Animate frame rotation clockwise.
210. **Digital roll counterclockwise** — Animate frame rotation counterclockwise.
211. **Virtual orbit** — Re-create a camera arc using 3D layers, depth, or a 3D scene.
212. **2.5D parallax push** — Move a virtual camera into separated foreground/background layers.
213. **2.5D parallax truck** — Move sideways across depth-separated layers.
214. **2.5D parallax rise** — Move upward through depth-separated layers.
215. **Simulated dolly zoom** — Approximate the vertigo effect using depth layers, scaling, or perspective warping.
216. **Virtual camera shake** — Keyframe small position/rotation changes to mimic handheld camera motion.
217. **Digital whip pan** — Fast horizontal image movement with motion blur.
218. **Digital whip tilt** — Fast vertical image movement with motion blur.
219. **Digital whip roll** — Fast rotating image move with motion blur.
220. **Ken Burns push-and-pan** — Combine slow scale and position changes across a still image.

---

**Terminology note:** *Truck/slide/track* can describe similar sideways travel; *crane/jib/boom* can overlap; and *push-in/pullback/dolly* are often used together. Zoom changes the lens framing and is not the same as physically moving the camera. Sections 9–10 are virtual or editing-simulated camera movements.
