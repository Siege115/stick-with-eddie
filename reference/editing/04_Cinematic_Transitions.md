# EDITING RESOURCES — CINEMATIC TRANSITIONS

**Reference focus:** Film and digital transitions for short-form edits, including Anime Breaker/Roblox, faceless videos, and CapCut workflows.  
**Entries:** 211 transition names, related variants, or practical transition combinations across 12 categories.  
**Important:** These are not all distinct standardized industry terms. Some are common film-language terms, others are descriptive editing techniques or context-specific recipes. This guide says *what the transition does* rather than pretending every variation has its own official name.

**Transitions vs camera movement:** A transition connects **two shots or moments**. A whip pan is a camera movement; a *whip-pan transition* uses a compatible pan to conceal a cut. A straight cut is also a transition, and often the strongest choice.

## How to choose quickly

- **Guide/information first?** Straight cut, insert cut, map-to-gameplay match, or simple J/L-cut.
- **Make two clips feel continuous?** Match-on-action, graphic match, screen-position match, or whip-pan transition.
- **Add drama?** Smash cut, impact-synced cut, or a short fade through dark.
- **Create a magical location change?** Portal reveal, object wipe, zoom blur, or brief light flash.
- **Make the viewer understand better?** Use fewer decorative transitions; preserve the important subject's screen position.

**Editing principles:** Keep transitions short in fast Shorts; match movement direction, timing, and subject location; use audio to reinforce rather than mask the idea. Test by watching once without sound and once with sound. Avoid frequent bright flashing or aggressive strobing.

## Practical CapCut workflow (iPhone)

1. Put the two clips side by side; trim out unnecessary gameplay first.
2. **For a hard, smash, match, or jump cut:** place the playhead at the exact visual moment and cut. For a match cut, position matching shapes/subjects similarly in both shots using crop/scale first.
3. **For a whip pan:** record or keyframe similar fast direction in both clips; add short directional motion blur around the cut. A premade preset alone may look disconnected if directions conflict.
4. **For an object wipe:** record an object crossing the full frame, or duplicate the foreground layer and animate a mask so the new shot is exposed behind it; feather slightly if needed.
5. **For a zoom:** keyframe clip A scaling toward a chosen screen position; keyframe clip B starting enlarged and returning to normal. Add motion blur sparingly and align the focal point across shots.
6. **For an audio bridge:** place a whoosh on moving transitions, a clean impact on smash cuts, or let the next scene's audio enter before the picture (J-cut).
7. Export a 9:16, 30 fps preview and check on your phone. A 0.15-0.35-second transition often reads well in a short, but timing depends on motion and information density.

## 3 Anime Breaker micro-sequences

**Secret boss location (6-12 s):** Start at landmark -> cut on movement to alley -> foreground/wall-pass transition -> boss reveal -> HP/reward insert. Use one stylized transition at most if a straightforward cut is clearer.

**Code update (8-15 s):** Show the reward/result first -> smash cut to code list -> insert cuts for redemptions -> J-cut voiceover into confirmation. Prioritize legibility over flashy wipes.

**Training arc highlight (8-20 s):** Defeat -> smash cut to training -> accelerating montage cuts -> speed-ramp cut into rematch -> hold the impact for recognition.

## Checklist before using any transition

- [ ] Does it connect two different shots or moments (rather than merely move the camera)?
- [ ] Is the important subject still easy to find after the cut?
- [ ] Is the movement direction, color, or shape matched when continuity matters?
- [ ] Does it make the video easier to follow or more exciting?
- [ ] Is it quick enough for the pace of the Short?
- [ ] Have you avoided unnecessary strobe/flash sequences?

**Glossary:** *Mask* = an alpha/shape boundary that determines what is visible. *Luma* = brightness information used to drive a reveal. *Motion blur* = image smear along a movement. *Match cut* = cut based on visual or conceptual similarity. *J/L-cut* = audio begins early or continues late relative to the picture cut.
