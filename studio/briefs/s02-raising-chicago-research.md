# s02-raising-chicago: research note (candidate for Short #2, alternate 2)

Status: idea stage, waiting for the owner's pick. Not fact-checked yet. Research: free web search, 2026-11-17.

## The true story
- 1850s **Chicago** was built on flat, swampy ground barely above Lake Michigan. Streets were deep mud and water had nowhere to drain.
- **1855:** the city adopts a sewer plan (engineer Ellis Chesbrough). The pipes go *on top of* the old streets, then the streets are filled in and raised several feet. Buildings now sit below street level, so they have to be lifted too.
- Buildings are raised on **jackscrews**: hundreds of men turn screws a little at a time, in step, and new foundations go in underneath.
- **1861: the Tremont House**, a brick hotel at Lake and Dearborn, is raised **about six feet** by the firm Ely, Smith & Pullman, reportedly with **~5,000 jackscrews** and **~500–1,200 men** (?). The hotel stays open: guests stay in their rooms and business goes on as usual.
- **Disputed:** George Pullman's personal role is often overstated, so credit "the builders" or "the firm". Some numbers (1,584 jackscrews, 27.5 inches) may refer to a different building.

## Hidden reason / payoff
The city wasn't lifting buildings for fun. It had to raise *itself* to fit sewers underneath, to get out of the mud.

## Story shape (draft)
- **Working title:** The City That Lifted Itself
- **Hook:** "They lifted a hotel six feet into the air while the guests were still inside."
- **"You see…"** Chicago was so flat and muddy that wagons sank in the street and the water had nowhere to go.
- **Conflict:** the hotel guests and owners laugh: "You'll drop the whole building!"
- **Rule of three:**
  1. They lift one brick building, and it works.
  2. They lift a whole row of shops (Lake Street, 1860, to verify).
  3. The Tremont House goes up while guests eat breakfast, and nobody spills their coffee. The coffee detail is invented colour, so either cut it or label it as a joke.
- **Payoff:** "Chicago raised itself, building by building, so the sewer pipes could fit underneath. It worked, and that's part of why the streets are higher today."

## World, sets and costumes
- 1850s Chicago street: mud, plank sidewalks, brick hotel facade, wagons, horses.
- Under the hotel: a trench with rows of jackscrews, workers turning in rhythm (a great repeating animation).
- Hotel dining room: guests at tables, chandeliers.
- Costumes: top hats, bowlers, bonnets, workers' flat caps and braces.

## Reuse from the existing library
Brick-wall and cobble textures, windows, shutters, gas lamps, cart wheel and hand cart, `top`/`bowler`/`flat`/`bonnet` hats, the crowd extras (laughing and pointing), and the explainer room for a sewer cross-section.

## Difficulty
**Medium.** It is mostly brick buildings, which are close to the Berlin courtyard kit. But the era (1850s–60s, Western, brick) is the closest of the three to Short #1, so it adds the least variety to the set library.
