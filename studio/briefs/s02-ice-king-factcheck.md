## Safety and sensitivity flags
1. **Frozen pond:** show only grown-up workers on thick ice, with no kids on the ice and no one falling in. It shouldn't look like a fun thing to copy. Keep saws and ice tongs in adult hands.
2. **People of Martinique and Calcutta:** in 1806 Martinique was a French colony that used slave labour, and Calcutta was under British East India Company rule. Many of the first buyers there were British residents. Don't show cheerful plantation scenes. Draw merchants and crowds respectfully, with no caricature, accent gags or "exotic" stereotypes. The art director should review this, as the research note already says.
3. **Invented dialogue** (merchant line, crowd laughs) is fine as dramatization. Never present it as a historical quote or put it in the newspaper prop.
4. Nothing scary or unsafe otherwise. The debtors' prison episode is dropped (row 35).

## Verdict
**CHANGES NEEDED.** These are small and wording-only; the story holds:
- Row 8: replace "nobody had ever had a cold drink" (false) with "almost nobody had ice".
- Row 6: give no 1806 tonnage in narration.
- Rows 10–11: quote only "slippery speculation", with no exact date, and keep the newspaper prop generic.
- Row 12: say "nowhere to keep it cold… it melted away", not "melts on the dock".
- Row 16: use hay, straw and rice husks, not coal dust, wheat chaff or bark.
- Rows 19 and 21: drop "pine", and don't date sawdust or call Calcutta the first sawdust trip.
- Rows 26 and 29: say "most of the ice is still there".
- Row 27: say "about 15,000 miles" or "halfway around the world".
- Row 33: say "one of his best customers".
