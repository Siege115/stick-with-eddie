# s02-ice-king: v2 fix list (owner feedback on the v1 video + QA FAIL)

Date: 2026-11-23. Source: the owner's notes after watching v1, plus `studio/reports/s02-ice-king-qa.md`.
Goal from the owner: **entertaining and easy to watch.** Every action readable, nothing floating, nothing rushed, a strong hook, more detail and more 3D motion.

## A. Owner's notes (in order through the video)
1. **Ice slides over water.** At the pond the block slides across open water. It must slide **on the ice sheet**: haul it out of the channel onto the ice, then slide it along the ice to the ramp.
2. **Saw-cut shot is too quick** (about 1 s). Hold the cutting long enough to understand it, **about 2.5–3 s**: a worker's two-handed saw strokes, the groove deepening, chips spraying, a crack sound.
3. **Loading onto the ship: an invisible rope carries the block.** Make it real: a **visible block-and-tackle** from the yard arm (rope, pulley, hook, ice tongs gripping the block) with **sailors hauling the rope on deck** in time with the lift, the block swinging slightly, then lowered into the hatch. Every rope must connect to something.
4. **"He sent it all the way to the Caribbean" is too fast and has no goal.** Give it about 3 s with a clear purpose: the brig leaves the wharf (sails filling, Eddie waving from the quay), then a short **route card**: a dotted line Boston → Caribbean with the ship moving along it, "Martinique" arriving.
5. **Top hat lift is clipped at one corner.** The whole hat must stay inside the character card (no cut edges), resting in the raised hand.
6. **The bird in that scene follows Eddie oddly.** Gulls must fly their own path (wide arcs across the sky) or sit on posts and rails, never tracking a character.
7. **Jug (kitchen):** the handle isn't attached. Attach it cleanly to the body. The sweat drops float around it: make **condensation beads on the surface** and slow **drips running down** the side to a small ring on the table.
8. **Water cup (hot port):** undetailed. Make a proper period tin or glass cup: rim, inner water surface, highlight, condensation beads, a small shadow.
9. **The pause before "His secret?" sounds like the video ended.** No dead air: keep ambience and music under it. Use a short held note or soft riser into the reveal (this overrides the earlier "silence drop" idea).
10. **The secret is rushed.** Give the payoff room (target about 8–10 s from "His secret?" to the end):
    - "His secret?": a beat on a covered crate in the hold, the lid starting to lift (curiosity);
    - "Sawdust": the sawmill, sawdust pouring off the saw, held long enough to see;
    - "the stuff sawmills threw away": workers shovelling sawdust into barrels and carts;
    - "packed thick around the ice to keep the heat out": the cutaway, slow, arrows fading, the push-in settling on the block (the loop frame).
    Read these lines at **normal pace** (not the brisk 1.16 speed); keep the earlier beats brisk if needed. Total may run up to **about 68 s**: the owner prefers clarity over rushing (note this in Decisions).
11. **More detail on backgrounds and objects** everywhere (props, textures, signage, plants, people doing small tasks).
12. **More 3D animation:** waves and foam, rocking ship, flapping sails and flags, chimney smoke, swaying palms, gulls flying, falling snow, sawmill wheel turning, crowd idle motion, camera moves with parallax and foreground elements. Every shot should have something moving.
13. **A good hook** for the first 2 seconds: the narration starts almost immediately, and frame 0 to 2 s is dynamic. The block **bursts up out of the dark water** with a splash and ice crack, tongs biting, the lantern glint sweeping across it, the camera pushing in low, and Eddie pointing toward the ship. It must be readable in the first frame and moving from frame 0 (12 Hooks: show the result, surprise visual).
