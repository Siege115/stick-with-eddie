# s02-ice-king: research note (candidate for Short #2)

Status: idea stage, waiting for the owner's pick. Not fact-checked yet. Numbers marked (?) disagree between sources.
Research: free web search, 2026-11-17. en.wikipedia.org and gutenberg.org could not be fetched in this session, so the notes below come from search summaries.

## The true story
- **Frederic Tudor** (Boston, 1783–1864) decided to sell New England winter ice in hot countries. Everyone thought it was a joke.
- **Feb 1806:** his brig (*Favorite*, per one source) leaves Boston for **Martinique** with ice cut from a frozen pond: **80 or 130 tons (?)**.
- The **Boston Gazette** mocks him: *"No joke. A vessel with a cargo of 80 tons of Ice has cleared out from this port for Martinique. We hope this will not prove to be a slippery speculation."* The middle wording varies between sources, so find the original.
- **Trip 1 flops:** the ice arrives, but nobody there knows what to do with it, and with no ice house it melts. Sources say it lost money.
- **Years of trial and error:** he tests packing the ice in rice chaff, wheat chaff, coal dust and bark, and settles on **pine sawdust**. Sawmills throw sawdust away, so it costs nothing. He builds insulated ice houses (Havana, ~1816, with thick wood-shaving walls).
- **1833:** he ships **~180 tons** of ice **16,000 miles** to **Calcutta**, arriving on 6 Sept 1833 after about 4 months. A large share survives; the "~100 tons" figure still needs a source. Calcutta becomes his most profitable market.
- Nickname: **"the Ice King"**.

## Hidden reason / payoff object
**Sawdust.** The waste from the sawmill is what keeps ice frozen across the equator.

## Story shape (draft for the scriptwriter, not final)
- **Working title:** The Man Who Shipped Ice to the Tropics
- **Hook (0–4 s):** "He filled a ship with ice from a frozen pond and sailed it to the Caribbean."
- **"You see…"** In 1806 there were no fridges. In hot countries, nobody had ever had a cold drink. Frederic Tudor thought he could sell them winter.
- **Conflict:** the Boston newspaper laughs at him in print: "No joke… we hope this will not prove a slippery speculation."
- **Rule of three:**
  1. Martinique: the ice arrives and nobody knows what it's for. It melts on the dock. They laugh harder.
  2. He tries packing it in straw, chaff and coal dust, and it still melts.
  3. Calcutta, 16,000 miles and four months away, the longest and hottest trip of all. The ship docks, the hatch opens, and the ice is still there.
- **Payoff (last line):** "His secret was sawdust, the stuff sawmills threw away, which kept the cold in all the way across the world."
- Dialogue moment: a Martinique merchant holding a dripping block says "…and what do I do with it?"

## World, sets and costumes
- **Frozen pond (night/blue):** ice sheet, saws, horses dragging blocks, snow, sheds. New build.
- **Boston wharf + brig:** ship hull, mast and rigging, gangplank, crates, a crowd laughing, newspaper close-up. New build. The ship is reusable for many sea stories.
- **Tropical harbour (hot orange light):** palms, white walls, market stalls, dripping ice. One set re-dressed for both Martinique and Calcutta (awnings, colours, signage).
- **Sawmill corner / ice house:** sawdust piles, wood walls (small).
- **Costumes:** Eddie as Tudor (long coat, **top hat**); dockworkers (flat caps, aprons); Caribbean and Calcutta merchants in period dress, drawn respectfully, with no caricature. An art-director review is required.

## Reuse from the existing library
Courtyard props (barrels, crates, hand cart, wheelbarrow, pitchfork, hay bales, buckets), `top`, `bowler` and `flat` hats, coats and aprons, the 16 crowd extras (laughing and pointing poses), gas lamps for the night wharf, and the warm/cool/spot lighting rigs. Ice blocks are simple boxes with a translucent material.

## Difficulty
**Medium-high.** The ship and the frozen pond are new builds. Tropical ports share one set, and props are mostly reused. About 4 locations, which suits the bible's "smooth transitions" (push into the hatch → out in the tropics).
