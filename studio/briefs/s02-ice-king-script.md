### Plain narration (for the voice)
He filled a ship with ice from a frozen pond. Then he sailed it to the Caribbean. You see, in 1806, there were no fridges. In hot places, almost nobody had ice. Frederic Tudor, from Boston, wanted to sell them winter. Boston laughed. A newspaper joked it might be a "slippery speculation." His ice reached the island of Martinique. But there was nowhere to keep it cold. *(Merchant: "Lovely. And… what do I do with it?")* It melted away, and he lost a lot of money. Boston laughed harder. So he tried packing the ice in hay. Then straw. Then rice husks. Too much of it still melted. Over many years, he found something better. In 1833, he sent a ship of ice to Calcutta, in India. About 15,000 miles. Four months at sea. His longest, hottest trip yet. The ship docks. The hatch opens, and most of the ice is still there. They called him the Ice King. His secret? Sawdust, the stuff sawmills threw away, packed thick around the ice to keep the heat out.
